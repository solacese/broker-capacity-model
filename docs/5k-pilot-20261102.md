# Focused 5K SDKPerf pilot, 2 November 2026

An owned, idle 5K Solace Cloud broker in AWS us-east-1 (broker version 10.26)
was tested with two nearby SDKPerf workers. All ten cases used SMF/TLS,
Persistent messages, four publisher connections, one consumer per queue,
255-message publisher/subscriber windows, and a full 300-second measurement
after warmup. Each case had its own drain and integrity check.

All ten trials reconciled whole-trial counters. Every subscriber received
exactly the messages transmitted, with zero missing, duplicate or CRC-invalid
messages; every queue was empty after drain. Accepted ingress variation was
under 0.8% in each trial. No sustained per-queue delivery deficit or growing
spool trend was classified. The native broker spool samples reported zero
disk-spooled messages throughout the pilot. These are **finite-horizon stable
lower observations**, not maximum throughput or proof that the broker never
uses a slower path. In particular, 20,000 msg/s at 1 KiB and fanout two is a
lower bound for that exact workload, not a 5K-class capacity number.

The four-core SDKPerf publisher worker showed approximately 100% host CPU even
at the lowest requested rate. It met every offered rate, so this is not proof
of a generator limit at the tested points. It does mean that a higher-rate
failure cannot be assigned to the broker without a larger publisher-worker
control. Subscriber host CPU remained low in this pilot. A second limitation is
that these observations do not supply independent broker limiting-resource
attribution or a repeatable upper endpoint.

The ten runs reserved about **$7.55** of modeled traffic and **$3.25** for the
four-hour two-worker lease. Together with earlier shared reservations, the
rolling modeled total was **$15.06** under the $16 operating guard, leaving
$4 headroom under the authorized $20/day generator/traffic limit. These are
conservative reservations, not an AWS or Solace invoice. Both exact run-tagged
workers were verified terminated after the pilot. The existing broker remains.

## Next measurement method

1. Fix one workload shape (start with 1 KiB, fanout two), broker version,
   consumer setup, ACK windows and topology. Use short, budgeted rate scouts
   above 20,000 msg/s to locate a bracket before paying for five-minute runs.
2. Confirm a sustainable lower rate and an unsustainable upper rate with
   independent 300-second trials. Require accepted ingress within 2% of target
   and stable within 5% during each window.
3. For each destination, pair a sustained egress shortfall with growth of
   **that same queue**. A falling egress rate and a flat egress plateau below
   ingress are separate transition candidates; neither alone is a validated
   broker limit. Preserve exact delivery, spool and integrity evidence.
4. Repeat the candidate workload with more publisher and subscriber capacity
   without changing the broker workload. If the edge moves, report the
   apparatus/path limit. If it does not, obtain independent broker resource
   evidence before using the bracket to train a capacity model.
